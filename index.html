Your Altamonte Springs emergency plumber, awake when the pipes are not

The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

1

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. In many homes it sits near the water meter at the street or beside the water heater. Open one faucet to drain the pressure.

2

Cut power near water

If water is near outlets, appliances or a breaker panel, switch off power to that area from a dry spot. Never stand in water to reach a panel.

3

Stop using drains

For a backup, do not run sinks, showers, dishwashers or the washing machine. More water has nowhere to go and will rise through the lowest drain.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call (407) 964-8940 and tell the dispatcher what you see. If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911, then your gas company.

Photograph the damage before you move anything. It helps if you file an insurance claim later, and it helps the technician see how the water traveled.

What brings Altamonte Springs homeowners to the phone

Hidden and active leaks

A meter that spins with every tap closed, a warm patch on a tile floor or a stain spreading on a ceiling all point to a leak. Leak detection finds it without tearing out walls.

Clogs and sewer backups

Slow drains, gurgling toilets and water rising in a tub signal a blockage. A technician can clear it with a cable or with hydro jetting when grease or roots are the cause.

Water heaters that quit

No hot water, a rumbling tank or a puddle at the base needs a fast answer. We compare repair with replacement before you spend anything.

Kitchen, bath and utility fixtures round out the list. A toilet that will not stop running, a disposal that hums and a supply valve that will not close are common after-hours calls.

What Altamonte Springs water and storms mean for your plumbing

The City of Altamonte Springs supplies drinking water, sewer service and reclaimed water. Its drinking water is groundwater from the Floridan aquifer, treated with tray aeration, chlorine and orthophosphate for corrosion control, according to the city. If you see staining or notice taste changes, the city's annual water quality report is the place to check before blaming your pipes.

The Little Wekiva River runs through town and reached record flood levels during Hurricane Ian on September 29, 2022, then overflowed again during Hurricane Milton in October 2024. After any flood, have a plumber check exterior cleanouts, the water heater base and low drains before you reuse them, and call the city's sewer line at (407) 571-8533 if the street sewer itself is backing up.

Call right now if you see any of these

  • Water spraying or pouring from a pipe, fitting or ceiling.
  • Sewage rising in a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • A water meter that keeps moving with every fixture off.
  • A water heater leaking, scorching hot at the relief valve or making loud banging.
  • A toilet overflowing that will not stop when you close the supply valve.
  • A strong rotten-egg smell near a gas appliance (leave first, call 911, then your gas company).
