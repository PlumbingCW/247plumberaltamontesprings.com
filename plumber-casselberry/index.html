Lake streets near Secret Lake Park and Lake Concord

The city counts more than 24 lakes inside its limits, with Lake Howell, Lake Kathryn and the three Triplet lakes among the largest. Secret Lake Park, with its boardwalk and two fishing piers, and Lake Concord Park, which surrounds City Hall, are the landmarks callers most often give us when they describe where they live.

Low lots beside water collect runoff, and a yard that stays soggy after a storm pushes water toward cleanouts and slab edges. After a heavy rain, lift the cap on any outdoor cleanout and look for standing water, then check floor drains and the garage corner by the water heater. Tell the dispatcher which lake or park is nearest so the truck finds the right street the first time.

Summerloch Green: three floods in six years

Summerloch Green, a 55-plus community, was hit by Hurricane Irma in 2017 and flooded again after Hurricane Ian in September 2022, according to news coverage. In November 2023 it took water after several days of rain, when a weir blocked with debris had to be opened by city crews.

If water has been inside your home, treat the water heater, outlets and gas appliances as unsafe until they are checked. Also ask a plumber to inspect floor drains, cleanouts and the sewer line for backflow before you go back to normal use, because floodwater can push sewage up through low fixtures.

Ridge Drive, Tanglewood and the meter line

On December 17, 2021, a water main broke on Ridge Drive in the Tanglewood subdivision near High Street. News reports say service returned around 4:30 that afternoon and the city did not need to issue a boil notice.

The city says it owns the main up to the meter, and the customer owns the pipe from the meter to the house. For a break on the street side, the city's main-break line is (407) 262-7725 Monday through Thursday from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. and (407) 262-7613 after hours. Everything between the meter and your walls is yours, and that stretch is where we work.

Which side of the meter is leaking

A few minutes at the curb tells you whether to call the city or us.

  • Find the meter box near the curb and lift the lid. Wet soil or bubbling on the street side of the meter points to the city.
  • Close the main valve where the line enters your house, then watch the meter's small leak-indicator wheel or dial for movement.
  • A dial that keeps turning with every fixture off means water is escaping between the meter and the house valve, which is on your side.
  • Look for a green, soft strip of lawn or a warm wet patch on a slab floor. Both can follow a buried line.
  • Photograph the meter reading and the wet area before anyone digs.

The tap water and the 2014 boil notice

Casselberry runs its own utility, drawing water from the Floridan aquifer through three treatment plants with three wells each. On December 25, 2014, a chlorine system malfunction led to a boil-water notice for about 27,500 customers north of SR 436 and Red Bug Lake Road, which was lifted on December 27.

After any notice, run cold taps for a few minutes and flush the ice maker line. Replace refrigerator and under-sink filter cartridges. We checked the city's 2025 water quality report and it lists sodium but not hardness, so we will not guess a number. A hardness test strip from a hardware store gives you your own reading.

Homes from every decade, and what the walls hold

Housing units in the city grew from 8,808 in 1990 to 11,803 in 2017, per a UF GeoPlan profile of Census data. That means roughly three of every four units standing in 2017 were already there in 1990, and about 2,400 more were added between 2000 and 2010. The city's timeline records neighborhoods platted as early as 1927 and 1928.

Age alone does not tell us which pipe is behind your drywall, so we look instead of assuming. The same timeline notes a sinkhole on Northmoor Road in May 1965 that swallowed a house. That is one old event, not a risk map, but new floor cracks or a sudden low spot in the yard deserve a call.
