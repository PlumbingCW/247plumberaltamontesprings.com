Sewer Line Repair Cost: Trenchless vs Open Trench and What Sets the Price

Sewer line repair commonly runs from about $1,500 for a small spot repair to $10,000 or more for a long replacement, and those are typical ballparks that change with the job. The big swing comes from method, depth, length, and what sits on top of the pipe.

A camera inspection comes first, because it turns a guess into a measured scope. This guide covers the two main methods, the factors behind the numbers, and who is responsible for which pipe. For a live problem, call (407) 964-8940.

Find out what is wrong before pricing the fix

A slow drain or a backup does not always mean the pipe is broken. A grease or root clog can be cleared for far less than a repair. A camera run through the cleanout shows cracks, offsets, sags, and root intrusion, with a footage count to the problem.

The footage matters because most repair prices are built per foot. A 6 foot broken section near the house is a different bill than 40 feet of failed pipe running under the driveway. Ask for the camera video and the footage marker where the damage starts.

Where a line is only clogged, main sewer line cleaning is the first stop and often the end of the story.

Signs the pipe may need repair, not just cleaning

Repeat clogs are the strongest clue. A pipe that is merely clogged clears and stays clear for months. A damaged pipe clogs again within weeks because roots, a crack, or a sag keeps catching waste.

Trenchless or trench: which one fits your pipe

When trenchless makes sense

Lining and bursting leave the lawn, driveway, and landscaping largely alone. They work best when the old pipe still holds a straight line. A liner follows the old pipe's shape, so it will not fix a belly where water pools.

When you still need a trench

A pipe that has sagged, separated, or lost its slope has to be dug up and reset. A trench also lets the crew bed the new pipe in clean sand and set a correct fall of about 1/4 inch per foot. Where the line runs under a slab or a large tree, the price can climb because of the extra work around it.

What actually moves the price

Two houses with the same footage can get very different numbers. Depth, soil, surface restoration, and local permitting each change the work. Here are the main drivers in plain terms.

Questions to ask before you approve a repair

A clear scope protects you from surprises. Ask each of these, and write down the answers.

  • Where exactly does the damage start and end, in feet from the cleanout?
  • Can I see the camera footage and a still image of the defect?
  • Is the quote for spot repair, lining, or full replacement, and why that one?
  • What is included for restoring the yard, driveway, or flooring?
  • Does the price include a final camera run to prove the pipe is clear?
  • Who pulls the permit and who is present at inspection?
  • What happens to the price if the crew finds a second defect?

City line or your line: how to sort it out

Your private lateral runs from the house to a connection on the city main. A backup that affects every drain at once, or a neighbor with the same problem, often points to the main. A single bathroom or kitchen drain backing up points inside the house or on your own line.

Before paying for repair, run a camera and note the footage where the blockage or defect sits. If the camera finds the defect close to the street, call the city sewer office with that measurement. If it is close to the house, the repair is usually your decision.

This pointer comes from how sewers are laid out in general, not from a published Altamonte Springs rule, so confirm with the city.

What Altamonte Springs homeowners should know about sewer ownership

Altamonte Springs runs its own sewer system, and its Sewer Collection page lists gravity sewers, force mains, manholes, and lift stations as the city's side. Nothing we read says who owns the lateral from your house to the main. Settle that before you pay for repairs, by asking the Sewer Collection office at (407) 571-8533.

For an active backup after hours, the city lists (407) 571-8686. A quick call can tell you whether the blockage sits in the city main, which would affect whether you need a private repair at all. Many of the city's 137 single-family subdivisions and 97 multifamily communities share these lines, so a manager may need to be in the loop.

A permitted repair is checked against the plumbing code, the IPC-derived plumbing chapters Florida enforces, so ask whether the quote includes the permit and the inspection.

If you are buying or selling a home

Many buyers add a sewer scope to the inspection. A camera run costs a fraction of a repair and can reveal a sag or root intrusion before closing. If you are already considering it, a sewer camera inspection gives you a written picture and footage to negotiate with.

A seller who finds a defect first gets time to compare methods and prices. A buyer who finds it late may be negotiating under a deadline.

Cutting the odds of a second repair

Roots find any gap, so a new liner or fresh pipe still benefits from yearly camera or cleaning checks in a tree-heavy lot. Keep grease, wipes, and paper towels out of the drain. If roots return and the line is sound, hydro-jetting clears them without opening the yard.
