How to Tell If a Plumbing Vent Is Clogged and Clear It

A clogged plumbing vent shows up as gurgling drains, toilets that bubble when another fixture runs, slow drainage in several rooms, and sewer smell indoors. The vent is the pipe that runs from your drain system up through the roof, and when it blocks, the drains cannot pull air.

You can often clear it from the roof with a garden hose and a plumber's snake. If the blockage is deep, or the roof is steep or wet, call a plumber.

What the vent pipe actually does

Every drain needs air behind the water. Without it, water leaving a sink pulls a vacuum and can suck the water out of nearby traps. The vent pipe, usually 1.5 to 3 inches wide, lets outside air in and lets sewer gas out through the roof.

Most houses have one or more vent stacks that poke through the shingles. Fixtures in a room often share a branch that joins the stack inside the wall. When the roof opening or the pipe is blocked, the whole branch behaves badly.

Signs your vent is blocked

One slow drain is usually a local clog. A pattern across fixtures points to the vent or the main line.

  • Gurgling from a sink, tub or toilet after another fixture drains.
  • A toilet that bubbles or burps when the washing machine empties.
  • Slow draining in several fixtures at once, with no clog found in the trap.
  • Sewer smell near a drain, because the water seal in the trap has been siphoned away.
  • A toilet that flushes weakly, with a sluggish swirl.
  • A drain that works for a few minutes after a snake clears it, then slows again.

Vent clog or main line clog: telling them apart

Both problems make drains gurgle. The location of the symptoms is the best clue.

What blocks a vent on a Florida roof

The vent opening is exposed to the weather, and in this area the usual culprits are leaves, pine needles, bird nests, paper wasp nests and the odd dead lizard or rodent. Mortar chunks and roofing debris can fall in during re-roofing. A cracked or collapsed pipe inside the wall is less common and needs a plumber with a camera.

Inspect the vent from the ground with binoculars first. If you see a nest or leaves in the opening, that is likely the cause and it is a quick fix.

How to clear the vent yourself, step by step

Only attempt this if the roof is dry, the pitch is low enough to walk safely, and someone is on the ground to steady the ladder. Wear rubber-soled shoes and keep three points of contact on the ladder. If the pitch is steep or you feel uneasy, stop and call.

Use a flashlight to look down the pipe. Pull out any visible debris with a gloved hand or a wire. Run a garden hose into the pipe and watch whether the water drains away quickly. If it backs up and stands, there is a blockage lower down.

Using a snake on a stubborn vent

A 25-foot hand-crank drain snake with a small head can reach a typical vent branch. Feed it slowly, rotate when you feel resistance, and pull it out with debris on the end. Never force it through a stack that bends sharply, since you can puncture the pipe.

After clearing, flush the hose again and then run water in the fixtures inside. If the gurgling stops, the vent is clear.

Sewer smell and gurgling can also come from a failing wax ring or a dry trap in a spare bathroom. Run water in unused drains monthly to refill the trap before you blame the vent.

When a plumber is the right call

Call if the vent clears and the gurgling returns within days, if the roof is steep, tile or wet, or if you suspect a break inside the wall. A technician checks the vent with a camera or a long cable, finds the blockage, and clears it from the roof or from a cleanout. They can also confirm whether the real problem is a clogged drain, which a drain cleaning visit would fix.

If the gurgling comes with water rising in a floor drain, the main sewer line may be blocked, and sewer line cleaning is the next step. Do not use chemical drain openers when you suspect a vent problem. They sit in the pipe and can damage it.

Vents on new work and remodels

A remodel that adds a bathroom or moves a sink needs a vent for the new fixtures, and a poor layout can cause the same gurgling you see in an old clogged vent. A typical rough-in has white PVC drains and vents running through the studs before drywall goes up.

If you are adding a bathroom, a licensed contractor handles the layout under plumbing installation work and arranges the permit. Ask the City Building and Fire Safety Department at (407) 571-8446 which permit applies to your project.

Keeping vents clear year after year

Trim branches that hang over the roof, and check each vent opening twice a year with binoculars. A small cap or screen is sold for some pipes, but a poorly fitted cap can trap leaves and block the vent, so ask a plumber before installing one.

If you live near Lake Mary or Casselberry, ask about a vent check at your next visit. Our crews serving Lake Mary and Casselberry can add it to any job, and tree trimming before leaf drop makes the check easier.
