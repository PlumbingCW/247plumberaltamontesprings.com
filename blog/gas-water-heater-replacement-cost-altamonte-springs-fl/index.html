How Much Does a Gas Water Heater Replacement Cost?

A typical gas tank water heater replacement often lands between about 1,400 and 3,500 dollars installed, depending on tank size, venting, and code work. A tankless gas unit usually costs more, often several thousand dollars. These are ballparks, not quotes.

Your own number depends on the heater you choose and what the old install needs to meet current code. The sections below break down each driver, show a table of typical ranges, and list what to ask before you say yes.

What drives the price of a gas tank replacement

The unit itself is only part of the bill. Labor, parts, haul-away, and any code corrections around the heater make up the rest. The table shows typical ballparks that change with the job.

Replacement typeTypical installed ballpark
40 gallon gas tank, standard ventingOften about 1,400 to 2,600 dollars
50 gallon gas tank, standard ventingOften about 1,600 to 3,000 dollars
Power-vent gas tankOften about 2,500 to 4,500 dollars
Gas tanklessOften about 3,000 to 6,500 dollars or more
  • Tank sizeA 40 gallon tank costs less than a 50 or 75 gallon. Choose by household size, not by habit. A family of four often needs 50 gallons or more.
  • Efficiency and featuresHigher-efficiency and power-vent models cost more up front and may lower the gas bill.
  • VentingA standard atmospheric heater uses a metal flue. A power-vent unit uses PVC and an electric fan, which needs an outlet and can add labor.
  • LocationA garage install is simple. An attic, closet, or second-floor install takes more time, and a pan and drain may be needed.
  • Code itemsA new shutoff, a sediment trap on the gas line, a relief valve and discharge pipe, a drain pan, and flex connectors may all be needed.
  • PermitAsk the City Building and Fire Safety Department whether a permit applies to your swap.

Ranges are typical ballparks only and change with the job. The price is stated clearly before work starts, once a technician has looked at the heater, the venting, and the gas line.

Tank, power vent, or tankless: what you pay for

The choice you make before the visit changes the total more than any other step. Here is how the three common gas options compare.

TypeUpfront costGood to know
Standard tankLowestNeeds a flue and combustion air, simple to service
Power-vent tankMiddleVents through PVC, needs an electric outlet, more flexible on location
Gas tanklessHighestEndless hot water within flow limits, needs a larger gas line and a vent upgrade

Signs the old heater is nearly done

Plan for a replacement before it fails and floods the garage.

A few of these signs together, such as age plus a wet base, mean you should price a replacement now. Write down the model and serial number from the label, and note the tank size in gallons, to speed up quotes.

  • The tank is 10 to 12 years old or more. The serial number often encodes the build date.
  • Water pools around the base of the tank, a sign the inner shell may be leaking.
  • Rusty hot water from the tap.
  • Rumbling or popping from sediment at the bottom of the tank.
  • The pilot light keeps going out, or the flame looks yellow instead of blue.
  • The relief valve drips or the area around the flue is scorched.

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

A leaking shell cannot be fixed, so that is a replacement. A bad thermocouple, thermostat, or gas valve is a repair, often a few hundred dollars. If the tank is past about 10 years and the repair costs more than a third of a new heater, replacement usually makes more sense.

If you are unsure, a technician can tell you in a single visit. A water heater repair call can sort out a pilot or valve fault before you spend on a new unit.

Your installer should explain how those apply.

What the installer does on replacement day

The crew shuts off the gas and water, drains the old tank, and disconnects the flue and lines. They set the new heater, connect new water lines and the relief valve discharge, reconnect gas with a new sediment trap if needed, and leak-test every fitting.

Then they relight the burner, check the draft at the flue, and set the thermostat. Most swaps take about 2 to 4 hours. Ask for a written line-by-line description of what is included. The details are covered in our water heater replacement service.

How to compare quotes fairly

Put the quotes side by side and check that each covers the same items. One may include a new gas shutoff and a drain pan, another may not. A low number that leaves out code items is not a bargain.

Ask these questions: Which brand and model? What is the warranty on the tank and on labor? Does it include permit handling, haul-away, new connectors, and an expansion tank if the system is closed? Check that the contractor holds a Florida plumbing license on the DBPR website, since unlicensed contracting is against state law.

Extras that can change the final bill

Some homes need a new drain pan and pan drain line. Others need a thermal expansion tank, a new gas shutoff, or a larger flue. Old flex connectors and corroded fittings are normally replaced rather than reused.

Haul-away of the old tank is sometimes included and sometimes billed separately. Ask what happens to the old heater and whether the quote lists each item, so there are no surprises on the day.

Switching from gas to electric or tankless

A gas heater can be replaced by an electric tank, but that usually needs a dedicated 240 volt circuit and a breaker, which adds electrician cost. Going to gas tankless often needs a larger gas line and new venting. Each change can add hundreds or thousands of dollars to the total.
