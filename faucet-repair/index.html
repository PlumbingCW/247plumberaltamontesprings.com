Water is spraying from a faucet or valve right now

Four moves limit the damage before anyone arrives. None of them needs a tool.

1

Close the stop valve

Reach under the sink and turn the small valve on each supply line clockwise until it stops. Hot is usually on the left and cold on the right. A quarter-turn handle needs only a 90 degree twist.

2

Shut the main if the stop will not turn

The main shutoff sits where the water line enters the house, often near a hose bib or in the garage. A gate valve takes several clockwise turns. A ball valve takes a quarter turn. Never force a handle that is frozen with scale.

3

Relieve the pressure

Open the lowest faucet in the house so the pipes drain down. Move rugs, empty the cabinet under the sink, and set a bucket under any drip.

4

Photograph the damage

Take pictures of the failed fixture, standing water and wet surfaces before you wipe anything. Your insurer will ask for them. Keep the old cartridge or valve if it comes off in your hand.

Then call (407) 964-8940 and say what is spraying, whether the stop valve closed, and whether the water looks clear. If water has reached an outlet, a light fixture or the electrical panel, stay out of the room and call 911 first.

Reading the drip, the hiss and the weak stream

A faucet has only a few moving parts, so the symptom usually points to one of them. Match what you see to the table before anyone picks up a wrench. The match also tells you whether a repair is realistic or the fixture is past saving.

What you noticeLikely causeTypical fix
Drip from the spout when the handle is offWorn washer and seat, or a failed cartridge or ceramic discReplace the washer and seat, or the cartridge
Water at the base of the spout or under the handleDry or cracked O-ringsReplace the O-rings and lubricate
Hissing or whistling when openWorn internal part, or a stop valve that is only partly openOpen the stop fully, then reseat or replace the cartridge
Weak flow from one faucet onlyClogged aerator or debris in the cartridgeClean the aerator and flush the cartridge

What to check before the technician arrives

Five minutes of notes helps the technician bring the right cartridge, O-rings and supply lines on the first trip.

  • Which faucet is failing and whether the problem is on the hot side, the cold side or both.
  • Where the water shows up: the spout, the handle, the base or underneath the sink.
  • The brand stamped on the handle or body, such as Delta, Moen or Kohler, or a photo of the model tag.
  • Whether the stop valves under the sink turn and close completely.
  • Whether the faucet has a pull-down sprayer or a separate side sprayer.

Kitchen, bathroom and laundry faucets: what gets repaired

A single-handle faucet runs on a cartridge, a brass or plastic cylinder that slides or rotates to mix hot and cold. Two-handle compression faucets press a rubber washer onto a brass seat. A failed ceramic disc cartridge usually costs far less to swap than a new faucet.

Supply lines and stop valves under the sink

The stop valve under the sink is the weak link, and the braided supply lines above it are rated for years, not decades. Older multi-turn stops seep around the stem or refuse to close after sitting open for years. The valve behind your toilet is the same design, and toilet repair often fits into the same visit.

Shower valves, showerheads and tub spouts

Behind the shower trim sits a valve body with a cartridge that mixes hot and cold. In a pressure-balancing valve, it also holds the temperature steady when a toilet flushes elsewhere. A showerhead that drips after the handle is off points to a worn cartridge or seat.

The trim plate comes off with a screwdriver, and the cartridge slides out once a retaining clip or nut is removed. If the valve body is cracked or the wall has no access panel, the opening is often cut from the back side of the wall, so ask where the cut will go before work starts.

Showerheads and tub spouts

Federal rules cap showerheads at 2.5 gallons per minute, so a head that sprays sideways or runs weak is usually plugged with scale. An hour in white vinegar often clears it. A tub spout that leaks from the diverter when the shower runs needs a new spout, which is a short job on most slip-on models.

Shower and tub drains that clog

A slow tub or shower drain is usually hair and soap wrapped around the stopper, not a faucet fault. Pull the strainer, lift out the hair and flush with hot water. If water rises around your feet, the blockage is deeper, and drain cleaning clears the branch line.

When the shutoff will not close

A stop valve that spins freely or a main that will not seat leaves water running with nothing to stop it. Do not wrench a corroded handle past its stop, because the stem can snap. Move to the next valve upstream: the main at the house, then the meter.

The meter and its valve belong to the city. Altamonte Springs Water Distribution, (407) 571-8340, maintains the pipes, valves and meters that deliver water to your property. If you still hear hissing at a wall after the faucet is off, leak detection with acoustic gear finds the pipe without opening drywall at random.

Mineral scale, aerators and the water that reaches your faucet

The City of Altamonte Springs supplies your water from the Floridan aquifer. It aerates the water to cut hydrogen sulfide and iron, adds chlorine, and doses orthophosphate for corrosion control. We could not confirm a hardness figure for the city, so do not assume one: unscrew an aerator, look for white crust, then use a hardness test strip or ask the utility for the number before you pick a faucet finish.

Over in Winter Springs, the city's drinking water page gives average hardness near 150 mg/L, about 8 grains per gallon, a level that leaves crust on aerators and shower cartridges. Compare what you see on your own fixtures before you decide the numbers match.
