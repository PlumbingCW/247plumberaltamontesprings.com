Emergency Water Line Repair in Altamonte Springs, FL

When the line that feeds your house breaks, you lose pressure or flood the yard, sometimes both. We answer 24/7, and while you wait you can close the shutoff on your side of the meter and keep people and pets off soft, saturated ground.

We find where the line is leaking, tell you whether a repair or a full replacement makes sense, and quote the work before we dig.

Why water pressure drops at every tap

A water service line leak is one of several causes of weak pressure, so it helps to rule others out. If only one fixture is weak, the cause is a clogged aerator, cartridge, or supply stop. If the whole house is weak, the usual suspects are a partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, a corroded galvanized line, or a leak in the service line.

A regulator that is too low or stuck can mimic a break, so we check pressure at an outdoor spigot with a gauge before we dig. A normal reading around 50 to 60 psi points away from the line. If the gauge is fine and old galvanized pipe is the culprit, a repiping job fixes the whole house.

Who owns which part of the line

The City of Altamonte Springs supplies the drinking water from groundwater drawn from the Floridan aquifer, and its Water Distribution Division maintains the pipes, valves, meters, and fire hydrants that deliver water to your property. The city site does not publish where its responsibility ends at each address, so ask Water Distribution before assuming a repair is yours or theirs.

Reclaimed water also moves through the same distribution system, so a lawn line that looks like a drinking water leak may be a reclaimed line. If you are not sure which one you have, tell us before we start digging.

Replacement methods compared

The best method depends on pipe condition, yard layout, and what sits above the line.

A replacement service line follows the plumbing code, the plumbing volume of the Florida Building Code, itself built on the IPC, and Florida's 811 rule requires a call at least two full business days before anyone digs.

MethodHow it worksFits when
Spot repairDig to the leak, cut out the damaged piece, add a couplingOne failure on an otherwise sound line
Open trench replacementExcavate the full run and lay new pipe in a clean trenchOpen lawn and easy access
Trenchless pull-throughDig two small pits and draw a new pipe along the old pathDriveways, patios, trees, and landscaping to protect
RerouteRun a new line along a different path, then retire the old oneOriginal path is blocked or unreachable

Finding a buried leak before we dig

A leaking line seldom leaves a clear wet spot in sandy Florida soil because water drains downward. We start with a pressure test from the house side, then listen along the route with acoustic gear. A tracer gas or a pipe locator fills in what the sound misses.

This matters because blind digging costs far more than a good survey. Our leak detection service covers the same tools in more detail, and we use them on every service line job.

Signs your service line is leaking

Watch for these around the yard and inside the house.

  • Green or unusually lush grass in a stripe between meter and house.
  • A wet or sunken area that stays damp during a dry week.
  • Water pressure that drops at every faucet and shower at once.
  • A meter that moves with all fixtures and the irrigation timer off.
  • Dirty or discolored water after a pressure drop.
  • A hissing sound near the meter box or where the line enters the house.

Planning new construction and added bathrooms

The same crew that repairs a line also handles new service connections for an addition, a guest house, or a remodel. Plan the route with the final grade in mind, since pipe depth and valve boxes are hard to move later. Larger jobs may need a permit, so ask the City Building and Fire Safety Department at (407) 571-8446.

Jobs for the city and jobs for us

We do not work on the city's mains, meters, or fire hydrants, and we do not dig without a locate ticket. A gas smell or a line strike is a 911 call first and then your gas utility's emergency number from your bill.
