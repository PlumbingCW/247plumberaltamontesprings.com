Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Altamonte Springs, FL

A sewer pipe that has cracked, sagged or collapsed shows up as repeated backups, soggy lawn or a sinkhole-like dip above the line. Our 24/7 line takes those calls. Until we arrive, use as little water as you can and keep everyone away from any wet or sunken ground.

Sewage backup that cleaning no longer fixes

If a line was cleared and backed up again within days, a physical defect is the likely cause. These steps protect the house while we come out.

1

Cut water use

Skip showers and laundry. Flush only when you must, and use a bucket of water to flush if the toilet is draining slowly.

2

Mark the wet spot

If the yard is soggy or has dropped, mark the area with a cone or a chair and keep mowers and vehicles off it.

3

Call before anyone digs

Do not start digging yourself. Florida requires a call to 811 at least two full business days before digging, and a sewer line can sit near gas or water lines.

4

Record what you see

Take photos of the backup, the wet ground and any cracks in the slab or driveway. They help the technician and any insurance claim.

Signs a sewer pipe has broken, sagged or collapsed

Repair is a different job from cleaning, so the signs matter.

  • Backups return within days or weeks of professional cleaning.
  • A wet, sunken or very green patch of lawn sits above the line to the street.
  • Sewer gas smell in the yard, garage or beneath a bathroom.
  • Several fixtures drain slowly with no clog found at the cleanout.
  • Slab or tile cracks run in a line from the house toward the street.
  • A camera has shown a sag, offset joint or missing pipe wall.

What a camera shows before any digging starts

We feed a camera on a push cable through the cleanout and record the whole run from the house to the connection. A crack looks like a dark line, an offset joint shows as a step, and a belly shows as standing water that never drains. Roots appear as pale fringe at the joints.

A locator on the camera head marks the surface directly above the problem, usually within a foot or so, and gives the depth. That means a trench can be dug in one spot instead of along the whole yard, and a sewer camera inspection can also be ordered by itself if you only want the findings.

Cast iron sewer pipe: what failure looks like

Cast iron is common in older buildings and under slabs. It rusts from the inside, building rough scale that catches paper, and in time it can flake through the wall. A camera shows a crust like rust on a bridge, and a hammer tap often sounds dull where the wall has thinned.

We cannot give you a safe-age number for cast iron in Altamonte Springs because no state or university source we reviewed supplied one. The honest test is a camera. If the wall is thick and round, cleaning may be enough. If it is flaking through, we repair or replace the section.

Lining a pipe without tearing up the yard

Cured-in-place lining pulls a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe, inflates it, and cures it hard in place. The result is a smooth pipe inside the old one, with roughly an eighth to a quarter inch less diameter. It works for cracks and root entry but needs a round pipe to begin with.

A sag or collapsed section has to be dug and replaced. Most lining jobs need the line cleaned first, often with a hydro jetting pass, so the liner bonds to the pipe wall.

Sandy soil, permits and a pointer on the city side

UF/IFAS describes soils in North and Central Florida as typically very sandy, and the Florida Geological Survey says limestone under the state can dissolve and cause sinkholes. A bedding of sand that washes out can leave a pipe unsupported. Ask for a camera view of the pipe bed if a dip forms over the line.

Permits in the city go through the Building and Fire Safety Department at (407) 571-8446, which lists a stand-alone plumbing permit type. Ask them what your repair needs, and ask the city's Sewer Collection office at (407) 571-8533 where your private line ends, because we found no published rule on that.

Building sewers are covered by the plumbing code, the plumbing volume of the Florida Building Code, itself built on the IPC. That is why slope, cleanout placement and materials are checked on a permitted repair.

Septic systems and a sewer connection check

Some Florida properties use a septic tank instead of a public sewer. The Seminole County Health Department regulates septic tanks, and a licensed septic contractor handles the tank and drain field. We handle the plumbing from the house to the tank, and the house side of a sewer connection, but we do not pump or repair a tank.

If you are not sure which system serves the house, check a bill from the city utility or ask the city. That one answer sets the whole scope of the repair.
