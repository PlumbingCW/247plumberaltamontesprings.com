Late May through the fall: what the wet season does to a drain line

The National Weather Service puts the median start of the wet season in central Florida at May 27, with the rains lasting about 142 days. Orlando, the nearest official station, averages 7.58 inches in June and 7.27 in July. Saturated ground sends water into the soil around buried sewer joints and cracks, and a line that ran fine in April can back up in a July downpour.

Before the rains settle in, find the cleanout cap in your yard and make sure it is not buried or cracked. Run water at the lowest tub or shower and watch how fast it drains. A slow drain in a dry week is the easiest time to clear a main, so call before the first storm instead of during it.

Lake Lily, Lake Catherine and the Victorian streets

The city traces its first deed to 1873 and its railroad to 1880. Houses along the Lake Lily and Lake Catherine corridor include the Wagner House from 1881, the O'Heir and Arch Houses from 1885, Chadburne Hall from 1890 and the Hill-Stone House from 1908. Maitland calls this a historical corridor, not a formal historic district.

Age tells you nothing certain about the pipes. Over 140 years of repairs, owners may have swapped sections, so a camera or an opened wall beats a guess. If you own an older house here, ask us to run a camera down the main before a remodel, and ask the city's permit staff before touching original structure.

Before we arrive in Maitland

1

Find the right shutoff

Water coming from a supply pipe stops at the main valve near the meter. Turn it clockwise. For a toilet or sink leak, the small oval handle under the fixture is faster.

2

Move valuables up

Lift rugs, electronics and cardboard boxes off the floor. Wet drywall starts to wick water up the wall within hours, so a few minutes of lifting now limits the damage.

3

Check the heater

A tank leaking at its base should have its cold supply valve closed and its power or gas control set to off. Do not touch a gas appliance that smells of gas. Leave and call 911.

4

Write down what you see

Note the time, the room and what you shut off. Take photos of the meter dial and the wet area so the plumber and your insurer both have a starting point.

A dispatcher answers the emergency line around the clock, including holidays, and can stay on while you work through these steps.

Dry spells, sandy soil and the Wymore Road sinkhole

On May 15, 2026, a hole opened on North Wymore Road near I-4 and Brightwater Circle. It first measured about 20 feet across and was later reported at 45 by 40 feet. Orange County Public Works repaved around 1,100 feet and reopened the road on July 2. The county told Spectrum News 13 that the aquifer, sandy soil and drought create conditions that favor sinkholes. The cause of that collapse was not stated.

The Florida Geological Survey says the whole state sits on carbonate rock that groundwater slowly dissolves. A leaking pipe can wash soil out from under a slab or yard. If you find a new dip in the lawn, a doorframe that has gone out of square or a floor crack that widens, call us to test the lines. Stay off any hole and call 911 if it is large.

Dommerich Hills: moving about 430 homes off septic

Dommerich Hills, in northeast Maitland beside a wetland and Lake Waumpi, was built in the 1960s with about 430 homes that each had two septic tanks. The city's sewer project connects them in phases. Phases 2 through 4 together cover about 340. Spectrum News 13 reported the owner fee at about $4,320, paid in one sum or over 10 years at 6.5% on the tax bill. We could not confirm the project's current status.

The city installs the main and a lateral stub toward each lot, and the owner reconnects the house. Maitland advises using certified and insured plumbing or septic companies for that work. Before the old tanks are abandoned, have them pumped, and ask us for a camera look at the house drain so the new connection is not made onto a cracked pipe.

Water heater swaps and what the permit counter asks

Maitland's published rules are specific here. A plumbing permit covers installing, repairing or replacing a water heater, and a like-for-like replacement in a single-family or duplex home can be handled over the counter.

  • Call the city's permit specialists at (407) 539-6150 before the job, or confirm that the contractor will pull the permit.
  • Ask to see the contractor's trade license, business tax receipt, liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage. The city requires them.
  • A notice of commencement is required when a project's value is over $5,000.
  • Keep the final inspection record. It matters when you sell or file an insurance claim.
  • Verify the license on the state's lookup at myfloridalicense.com, which separates certified plumbing contractors from registered ones.
