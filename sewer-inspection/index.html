Camera inspection

Sewer Inspection in Altamonte Springs, FL

A sewer camera answers a question that a cable cannot: what is the pipe actually like inside. We feed a waterproof camera through your cleanout, watch the live view, record the run and mark the location of any problem on the surface.

Most inspections are planned, such as a home purchase or a recurring slow drain. If a failure turns urgent, the same 24/7 line handles it.

What the camera sees inside your pipe

The camera head is about 1 to 2 inches across and rides on a flexible rod up to 200 feet long, with lights around the lens. A transmitter in the head lets a handheld locator find it from above ground, so the depth and position of each defect are known.

On screen, a healthy pipe looks like a clean, round tunnel with even joints. Trouble shows up as roots at the joints, a crack running along the pipe, a dark band of standing water, scale on cast iron, or a section that is oval or missing. We pause on each one and note the distance from the cleanout.

Good reasons to schedule an inspection

Plenty of people wait for a backup. These situations are better handled earlier.

  • You are buying a house and want to see the line before the inspection period ends.
  • A drain line has needed cleaning twice in a year.
  • You see a wet or sunken stripe of lawn between the house and the street.
  • The yard has large trees close to the sewer path.
  • A cable stopped at the same distance every time.
  • You are planning a remodel that adds a bathroom or moves a drain.

Camera work for buying or selling a home

A standard home inspection does not look inside the sewer. A camera report before closing can reveal a root-filled line, a belly or a collapsed section that the seller may not know about. With a recording in hand, a buyer can ask for a repair, a credit or a price change.

The city counts 7,392 single-family parcels in 137 subdivisions and 17,110 multifamily units, and about half of the 21,196 housing units are renter-occupied. That mix means inspections serve buyers, sellers, landlords and property managers. Where the city's line ends and yours begins

Public sewer mains in town belong to the city, which you can reach through Sewer Collection at (407) 571-8533, or at (407) 571-8686 after hours. Its published pages do not say where a private lateral ends. Call with your address before you assume.

A camera can help in that conversation. We record the distance from the cleanout to the point where your pipe joins the next one, and that number gives the city something to point to. We do not enter the public main.

What your report includes

You receive a video file of the run, still photos of each defect and a written summary of distances, depth and condition. We list what we saw in plain words and rank each item as monitor, clean or repair.

A recording is not a repair quote, but it makes one easier to get right. If the camera shows only grease or roots, main line cleaning is the next step. If it shows broken pipe, a sewer line repair estimate comes next, based on the same footage.

Roots, bellies and offsets in Central Florida pipe

Sandy soil drains fast and can shift under a pipe, and UF/IFAS describes soils in North and Central Florida as typically very sandy. That can leave a joint hanging and produce an offset or belly. Roots seek the moisture that leaks out at those joints.

The wet season starts around late May by the National Weather Service median, with roughly 142 days of heavy rain. A camera run in the dry months shows where the pipe holds water, and the rain later tests the repair. If you are weighing a house just up the road in Lake Mary, the same steps apply.

Limits of a camera and when the line is urgent

A camera shows the inside of the pipe but cannot see through a collapse or a full blockage. We say what we could not reach. If sewage is rising in a floor drain, the visit becomes an urgent call, and the 24/7 line takes it, but gas smells or sewage near electrical equipment call for 911 or your utility first. We also do not inspect the city's main.
